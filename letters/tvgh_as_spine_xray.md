Dear Dr Tsai,

I study the application of computer science to improving spinal health, and my current work needs cervical and lumbar spine radiographs from a Taiwanese centre; the spine radiograph sets I hold come from Thailand, Vietnam, Jordan, the United States and Europe. Your Spine X-Ray Images Dataset for Ankylosing Spondylitis on the Taiwan medical data portal (data.dmc.nycu.edu.tw/dataset/d08-x-ray) provides paired cervical and lumbar series in DICOM from Taipei Veterans General Hospital, and the fused and bridged spines of ankylosing spondylitis are among the hardest cases for telling one vertebra from the next.

My work measures how reliably automated image-analysis methods recognise which vertebra is which on radiographs, including the transitional and altered anatomy behind wrong-level errors in spine care, across devices and populations, reported as per-image agreement with expert labels and confidence intervals.

The portal shows no files to a visitor, so I am writing to ask whether the radiographs could be shared for non-commercial research, and what terms you require. I would also be grateful to know whether the TVGH-SpineXR set with vertebra-level labels (IEEE DataPort, doi:10.21227/x6bb-7r75) could be shared under the same terms; I have written separately to Dr I-Yun Lisa Hsieh about it. The images would be held on Wayne State University's research computing storage, read only by me, never redistributed, and deleted at the end of the project if you ask; the work is on de-identified imaging under Wayne State IRB determination HPR 2026-269. I will sign a data use agreement through Wayne State on your terms, cite the dataset as you direct, and share our results on your images with you before publication.
