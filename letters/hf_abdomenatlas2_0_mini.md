Body:

```text
Dear Professor Zhou,

I study the application of computer science to improving spinal health, and my current work needs abdominal and thoracolumbar CT from many centres and scanners, in which the vertebral levels can be counted, transitional anatomy included. AbdomenAtlas 2.0 Mini (10,135 CT on Hugging Face, AbdomenAtlas/AbdomenAtlas2.0Mini) provides that breadth, and AbdomenAtlas 3.0 Mini (9,262 CT) is already held on our storage.

My work measures how reliably automated image-analysis methods recognise which vertebra is which on CT and on radiographs, including the transitional variants that cause wrong-level errors in spine care, across modalities, devices and exposures, reported as per-image agreement with expert labels and confidence intervals, by device and by variant.

The 2.0 Mini release has no card or metadata, so before fetching its 620 GB I am writing to ask whether you could share the case list: for each BDMAP identifier, whether it is the same case as in 3.0 Mini and the public collection it comes from. That would let us fetch only the roughly 870 cases we do not yet hold. The images would be used for non-commercial research under the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licence, held on Wayne State's research computing storage, read only by the study team and never redistributed; the work is on de-identified imaging under Wayne State IRB determination HPR 2026-269. I work at the Department of General Surgery, Wayne State University School of Medicine and the Detroit Medical Center. We will cite your papers and the release in any publication and share our results on these images with you before publication.

With thanks,

Gregory J. Schwing, MD, PhD
Department of General Surgery
Wayne State University School of Medicine / Detroit Medical Center
Detroit, Michigan, USA
```
