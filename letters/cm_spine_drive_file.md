## The set, and why a letter

The paper's data availability statement says the code and data are at github.com/zengyuyuyu/Neighbor
(accessed on 19 March 2024). The repository's README linked the set as one Google Drive file
(id 1r9wX1802gkU4-EQHMMlyAlEiWXGWLhhJ) and the repository held code alone. On 2026-10-23 the
repository is no longer public and the Drive file does not open from the owner's Google account,
and no copy of the set is on any public hub or in any dataset list that was searched. The paper
carries no ethics approval or consent statement, so the letter asks for the terms and the approval
as well as the files. The owner had already written to the three authors on 2026-09-10 and
2026-09-20 about the dead Drive link, with no answer, so the letter says so, and the note to the
editorial office went the same day instead of two weeks later. Nothing to sign until the authors
answer.

Subject:

```text
[XR/CT Imaging Research] Request for the CM Spine images (Electronics 2024, 13, 2137)
```

Body:

```text
Dear Dr Cheng and Dr Song,

I study the application of computer science to improving spinal health, and my current work needs lumbar images taken during surgery with a mobile G-arm, the setting in which wrong-level errors happen. Your paper in Electronics 2024, 13, 2137 describes the CM Spine set: 208 anteroposterior lumbar images from a Geelin500-A G-arm, each lumbar vertebra labelled L1 to L5, 537 vertebrae in all. The paper's data availability statement points to github.com/zengyuyuyu/Neighbor, whose README linked the set as a Google Drive file; today the repository is no longer public and the Drive link does not open, so I am writing to you directly. I wrote to you about this on 10 and 20 September 2026; forgive me for writing again.

My work measures how reliably automated image-analysis methods recognise which vertebra is which on intraoperative images, including the transitional variants that cause wrong-level errors, across devices and exposures, reported as per-image agreement with expert labels and confidence intervals, by device and by level.

I am writing to ask whether the 208 images and their labels could be shared for non-commercial research, and what terms you require; it would also help to know the ethics approval under which the images were collected and that they are de-identified. I am at the Department of General Surgery, Wayne State University School of Medicine and the Detroit Medical Center, where the images would be held on Wayne State's research computing storage, read only by the study team, never redistributed, and deleted at the end of the project if you ask; the work is on de-identified imaging under Wayne State IRB determination HPR 2026-269. I will sign a data use agreement through Wayne State on your terms, cite your paper, quote the agreement in any publication, and share my results on your images with you before publication.

With thanks,
```

the office, and asks a reader to write to the corresponding author first, which the owner's
September emails did; the ethics team (publication.ethics@mdpi.com) is the next step if the office
does not answer.
